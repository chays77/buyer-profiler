# Examples — Buyer Profiler

Four examples showing the specialist in action. Each starts with a transcript excerpt and ends with a full profile + close strategy output. The first three are single-buyer calls. The fourth is a multi-buyer call showing the Decision Dynamic output.

---

## Example 1 — The Commander (High Confidence)

**Input:**

> "Look, I've been through three agencies in two years. I don't need a strategy deck. I need someone who can run it and show me the number. What's your close rate on leads you generate? And what does the engagement actually look like — I don't want a retainer where I'm guessing what I'm paying for."

**Output:**

---

### Buyer Profile

**Type: Commander (ENTJ)**
Confidence: High

**Cognitive stack:** Te-Ni-Se-Fi — leads with metrics and external logic, backed by a long-range vision; values sit underneath and surface late. Decides on what's measurable first, what's strategic second, what feels right last.

**Evidence:**
- Opened with track record of past failures — Te audit pattern. Commanders test the system before they trust it.
- "I don't need a strategy deck" — Te rejecting process theater. They want output and a way to measure it, not methodology.
- Asked for a specific metric (close rate) before any relationship signal. Te dominant, Fi inferior — data before any feeling-layer engagement.
- "I don't want to guess what I'm paying for" — Te accountability frame. They will hold scope to the number.

**Primary driver:** Fire — drive to acquire. Win-oriented, measuring you against ROI velocity.

**Blend notes:** Commander with Executive (ESTJ) process streak — they have the strategic-horizon lens of a Commander but they're also showing appetite for proven-process accountability (an Executive trait). Not pure strategic horizon — they want a plan they can audit.

**Inferior function blind spot:** Inferior Fi means they'll override their own values for the spreadsheet. They won't tell you they're personally uncomfortable with a vendor — they'll just keep grinding until something breaks. If you sense values misalignment in this deal, surface it explicitly — they don't have the muscle to surface it themselves.

**Failure mode for this deal:** Over-explaining your process or leading with relationship before value. They'll interpret it as Te-weak (insecurity, filler). The deal dies in the first three minutes if you pitch yourself before answering their question.

---

### Sandler Frame Check

| Dimension | Status |
|---|---|
| Pain | Partially identified — past agency failures are symptoms. Real cost (revenue loss, time, internal frustration) not yet quantified. |
| Budget | Unknown — not discussed. |
| Decision | Likely sole decision-maker based on language ("I don't want...") — confirm. |
| Control | They're running the frame right now. Need to reclaim it. |

**Critical gap:** Pain needs a dollar number. Budget must be surfaced before proposal.

---

### Close Strategy

**Open the next call with:**
> "Before I answer your question on close rates — I want to make sure those numbers mean something to you. Walk me through what you've been losing while this hasn't been solved. Not the frustration — the actual cost. Revenue, time, whatever you can put a number on."

**Why:** Gets them to own the pain before you pitch. Once they've said the number, your ROI frame has a reference point.

**On the close rate question:**
Answer it directly and briefly. Then redirect: "That number only matters in context — it depends entirely on the leads we're feeding it. What does your current lead quality look like?"

**Budget conversation:**
> "I want to make sure I build you something that's sized right. What does this problem need to be worth solving before you'd commit to fixing it?"

**Next step language:**
> "Can we get 30 minutes on Thursday? I'll bring two options — I want your reaction before I invest in building anything."

**What to hold back:**
Don't send a proposal unsolicited. Don't over-detail scope in writing before a second call. Commanders buy from people who don't over-explain.

## Example 2 — The Logistician (Medium Confidence)

**Input:**

> "We've been talking internally about this for about six months. We're not in a rush, honestly. We want to make sure we understand exactly what we're getting before we move forward. How does your onboarding process work? And how do you handle reporting — like, what does the monthly cadence look like?"

**Output:**

---

### Buyer Profile

**Type: Logistician (ISTJ)**
Confidence: Medium — single interaction, no pushback moments observed to confirm Te auxiliary

**Cognitive stack:** Si-Te-Fi-Ne — leads with proven process and historical precedent (Si), executes through metrics and external logic (Te). Inferior Ne means they distrust novel approaches and need precedent before trust. They will not move on something unproven, no matter how good the pitch.

**Evidence:**
- "Talking internally for six months" — Si dominant pattern. Long evaluation cycles, full due diligence before commitment.
- "Not in a rush" — Si trusts proven path over urgency. Not a disinterest signal — it's the function speaking.
- Asked about onboarding process and reporting cadence before outcomes — Si-Te ordering. Establish the proven process first, then the metrics that hold it accountable.
- "Understand exactly what we're getting" — Si needs the full known picture before committing. Inferior Ne means they will reject anything that feels speculative or unproven.

**Primary driver:** Earth — drive to defend. Process, stability, read the fine print.

**Blend notes:** Possible Defender (ISFJ) secondary lean — "talking internally" hints at group-comfort consideration surfacing alongside the process-first orientation. The two types share the same proven-process anchor; the difference is whether the secondary lens is metrics (Logistician) or group harmony (Defender). Probe: do they reference the team's comfort level, or only their own evaluation criteria?

**Inferior function blind spot:** Inferior Ne means they will reject novel approaches even when proven elsewhere. If your pitch leans on "this is a new way" — even with results — they'll go quiet. Lead with precedent. Map your approach to something they already trust before introducing what's different about it.

**Failure mode for this deal:** Moving too fast or being vague about your process. They will not tell you they're uncomfortable — they'll just go quiet. The deal dies slowly, not loudly.

---

### Sandler Frame Check

| Dimension | Status |
|---|---|
| Pain | Not surfaced. They've described a decision process, not a problem. Pain is unknown. |
| Budget | Unknown. |
| Decision | Unknown — "we've been talking internally" suggests a group. Who has final authority? |
| Control | They're in evaluation mode. Process questions are theirs to ask right now. Let them. |

**Critical gap:** Pain has not been named. Six months of internal discussion without urgency is a risk — they may be window shopping or need an internal event to create pressure.

---

### Close Strategy

**Do not rush this call.** Logisticians buy on thoroughness, not urgency. Answer every process question completely.

**Then redirect to pain:**
> "Happy to walk through all of that — I want to make sure we're solving the right problem first. What's been driving the six months of internal conversation? What would change for you if this was working well?"

**On decision:**
> "When you say 'we've been talking internally' — who else is part of the decision when it comes time to move forward?"

**On timeline:**
Don't push. Do anchor: > "What would need to be true for this to move forward in the next 60 days versus continuing to evaluate?"

**Next step language:**
> "I'll send you a one-pager on how our onboarding and reporting works — specifically what you'd see in month one. Does it make sense to schedule a follow-up once you've had a chance to review it?"

## Example 3 — Thin Transcript (Low Confidence, Probe Map)

**Input:**

> "Hey, yeah — someone referred you. We're a 12-person agency, doing about $4M. We've been trying to figure out our paid media situation. Sounds like you might be able to help."

**Output:**

---

### Buyer Profile

**Type: Insufficient signal — working hypothesis only**
Confidence: Low

**What's observable:**
- Referral-driven = relationship matters (F lean possible, or simply low-friction entry)
- "Figure out our paid media situation" is vague — no urgency language, no pain named
- "$4M" was offered without prompting — either transparency instinct (high I / Water lean) or anchoring for scope (Fire lean)
- Short statement ending in "sounds like you might be able to help" — deferring, not directing. Either S lean or early-stage trust-building

**Cannot determine:** T vs. F, J vs. P, decision authority, urgency level

**Working hypothesis:** Possible Defender or Consul (ISFJ/ESFJ) based on referral reliance and open deferral. Hold loosely.

---

### Probe Map (Next Call)

Ask these to sharpen the read:

| Question | What it reveals |
|---|---|
| "Walk me through what's been happening with paid media that made you start looking." | Pain depth, urgency, T vs F response style |
| "What would a win look like for you 90 days from now?" | Outcome orientation — data vs. relationship vs. feel |
| "How do you typically make a decision on something like this?" | Decision process, J vs P, who's involved |
| "What's your team's relationship been like with outside vendors?" | Trust baseline, S lean confirmation, risk tolerance |

**Close strategy:** Hold. No proposal, no framing recommendations until a second call produces signal. Your only job on the first call is to ask the probe questions and listen.

## Example 4 — Multi-Buyer Call (Decision-Maker + Implementer)

**Input:**

> Monthly check-in call with an existing client — an event ticketing SaaS company. Two buyers on the call: the founder/CEO ("M") and the marketing manager ("J"). The agency has been running paid media for them for ~12 months. M is direct, fast-moving, and announces several decisions live: switching ad spend to a new vendor's funnel system, considering canceling HubSpot ($2,500/month), wanting to rebuild a slow website. J brings up the agenda items collaboratively, defers to M on substance, but owns day-to-day execution.

**Output:**

---

### Buyer 1 Profile

**Type: Entrepreneur (ESTP)**
Confidence: High — multiple unguarded moments, hard numbers, decisive language

**Cognitive stack:** Se-Ti-Fe-Ni — leads with what's tangible right now (Se), tested through quick internal logic (Ti). Inferior Ni means he doesn't naturally hold a long-term strategic vision — he trusts what he can see working today and adjusts in real time.

**Evidence:**
- "It's do or die for a lot of companies" — Se urgency, action framing
- Already moved on a new vendor and an acquisition before this call. Se acts on what's in front of him.
- Hard numbers in the opener: $80 cost-per-demo, 1-in-4 close rate, $400 CPA vs. $1,400 average. Se measures what's tangible in dollars right now.
- Self-aware about expertise gaps: "I used to put WordPress sites together but my expertise dies after WP Optimization" — Ti precision about his own boundaries
- Tells you what's working *and* what he's fed up with, unprompted (HubSpot at $2,500/month). Low filter — Se reports reality without the Fe-buffer.
- Decisive: "We probably want to stay away from Meta with you guys for a while" — Se decides on what's working *now* (the new vendor's funnel results) and acts immediately.

**Primary driver:** Fire — drive to acquire. ROI-velocity-measured.

**Blend notes:** Entrepreneur with Debater (ENTP) lean — he has the internal-logic appetite to test ideas out loud and welcome counter-positioning when it's grounded in real numbers. Not just an action-only operator.

**Inferior function blind spot:** Inferior Ni means he reacts to short-term results faster than he holds long-term strategy. The Meta-vendor switch is exactly this: he saw better short-term CPA from one vendor and made the call without weighing the long-term portfolio risk. Your leverage is to bring the strategic horizon he's not naturally generating — but ground it in present-moment evidence (Se), not abstract framework.

**Failure mode for this deal:** Process-talking him to death. He announced a Meta budget reduction in this call and the seller didn't engage it. If you push back without offering a sharper alternative *grounded in tangible numbers*, the budget walks. He also flagged HubSpot frustration twice — Se reports reality, and if you don't engage the signal, you miss a renewal moment that's not actually about the website.

---

### Buyer 2 Profile

**Type: Protagonist (ENFJ)** with Consul (ESFJ) blend
Confidence: Medium — fewer direct quotes but consistent relational pattern

**Cognitive stack:** Fe-Ni-Se-Ti — leads with group harmony and others' needs (Fe), with vision underneath (Ni). Inferior Ti means she's slower to challenge ideas on pure logic — she trusts the room and the relationship over the framework.

**Evidence:**
- Opens by thanking the agency for being flexible so M could join — Fe maintaining the relationship layer
- "He can provide more business details… explain the vision better" — Fe deferring on substance to keep collaborative framing intact
- Uses "you guys" repeatedly — Fe-inclusive, group-decision orientation
- "There's so much I know there's so much more we could be doing… I don't even know we should be doing" — comfortable saying she doesn't know (Fe over inferior Ti — won't pretend the framework is solid when it isn't)
- "Do you want to talk about that, M?" — Fe checks in with the decision-maker rather than seizing the floor

**Primary driver:** Water — drive to bond. Relationship-led.

**Blend notes:** Protagonist with Consul (ESFJ) lean — both lead with group harmony. The difference is what backs that up: a Protagonist's secondary lens is forward-looking vision ("where could this go?"); a Consul's is what's worked before. Her language ("there's so much more we could be doing") points to the vision lens, but the deference pattern is the group-harmony driver either way.

**Inferior function blind spot:** Inferior Ti means she will not push back on the *logical* foundation of a decision — even one she's uncomfortable with — if pushing back disrupts the room. If M makes a call she has implementation concerns about, she won't object on logic grounds in the room. She'll execute and hope it works, or quietly slow it down. Your leverage: give her a private channel to surface logical concerns *outside* the M-dominated meeting.

**Failure mode for this deal:** Pushing recommendations through M without including J. She executes day-to-day. If she feels excluded — Fe will read that as relational disregard — the work stalls in implementation even after M says yes.

---

### Decision Dynamic

**Who has the actual veto:** M. He's the owner-operator. He made the Meta call solo on this transcript.
**Who runs implementation:** J. She's named the LLM strategy, brought up the GoHighLevel integration question, and manages day-to-day execution.
**The pairing risk:** M moves fast and decides in the room. J builds consensus and executes. If you only sell to M, J may feel bulldozed and slow-walk. If you only sell to J, you can't get a final decision.
**Tactical implication:** Frame the next move as two parallel tracks — one M can decide on the spot (strategic direction), one J owns end-to-end (execution detail). Gives M closure and J ownership simultaneously.

---

### Sandler Frame Check

| Dimension | Status |
|---|---|
| Pain | Multiple pain points named and partially quantified — Meta CPA $1,400 vs. $400 with new vendor, slow website, HubSpot at $2,500/month feels overpriced. |
| Budget | Active spend across multiple channels. Not budget-constrained. |
| Decision | M has authority. J runs implementation. Both must agree before scope expands. |
| Control | Mixed — they drove the agenda, the seller responded reactively. M announced decisions the seller didn't engage. |

**Critical gap:** M made a *unilateral decision* in this call ("stay away from Meta with you guys for a while") and the seller didn't engage it. That's a budget reduction announced live without a counter-frame. The deal isn't dying — the scope is shrinking in real time.

---

### Close Strategy

**The most important move on the next call:**

Reclaim the Meta budget question. M announced a decision the seller didn't engage. You have one chance to reframe before it becomes the new normal.

**Open the next call with M:**
> "M — I've been sitting with what you said about Meta. Before we accept that as the path, I want to make sure we're comparing the right things. Your vendor's funnel system is winning because the data loop back to Meta is tighter — not because their creative is better than ours. If we replicated that data loop on PMax with the same landing system, would you want to pressure-test whether we get similar gains on Google before we cede Meta entirely?"

**Why:** Honors his decision (doesn't argue with the result) while reframing the *cause* of the result. Entrepreneurs respect counter-positioning when it's grounded in their own logic.

**On the HubSpot question (engage M directly):**
> "You mentioned HubSpot twice — let's actually price out what GoHighLevel + a clean WordPress would cost. If the math works, you save $2,500/month. I'd rather we redirect that into ad spend than lose it to a vendor that isn't earning it."

**For J — schedule a working session separately:**
> "J, can you and [account manager] schedule a working session to walk through the GoHighLevel setup and the new ad funnel? I need to see exactly what's running before I can speak to whether we replicate it on PMax."

**Next step language for both:**
> "Give me until [specific date] to come back with a site audit report and a recommendation on the HubSpot question. Then let's get all four of us on a call to make those decisions together."

**What to hold back:** Don't agree to a website rebuild on the call. M is asking if a clean WordPress reimport solves the speed problem — that's a $5K-$10K project framed as a quick fix. Probe first: site speed problems on aged WordPress sites usually come from 3-4 specific issues. A clean reimport doesn't fix them — it reimports them.

**Why this works for both buyers:** M gets a specific deliverable on a specific date (his preferred mode). J gets a working session (her preferred mode — collaborative, hands-on). Both decisions get made together, which protects the relationship.
