# Signal Reading Guide — How to Type a Buyer From a Transcript

Use this to decode what a prospect is telling you about who they are — not from what they say, but from how they say it.

---

## The First 60 Seconds

Most buyer type reads are available in the first minute of an open-ended question. Ask "Tell me what's going on" and then listen for these signals:

**How long is their answer?**
- Short, punchy, directive → High D / Fire / Commander-type
- Long, contextual, story-driven → High I or S / Water or Earth lean
- Methodical, sequential → High C / Earth / Logistician-type
- Energetic, jumping between ideas → High I / Fire-Water blend / Campaigner-type

**What do they lead with?**
- Problem and cost → T lead, Fire or Earth driver
- Relationship or team dynamics → F lead, Water driver
- Process or history → S/J lead, Earth driver
- Possibilities or vision → N lead, Air driver

**Do they ask questions or make statements?**
- Statements first, then questions → E lean (Extrovert), high confidence
- Questions first, information-gathering → I lean (Introvert), processing before committing
- Pointed questions early → T lead, analytical orientation
- Open-ended questions → F lead or exploratory orientation

## Behavioral Signals by Category

### T vs. F (Thinking vs. Feeling — how they decide)
| T signals | F signals |
|---|---|
| Leads with data or metrics | Leads with team impact or relationship dynamics |
| Asks "what's the ROI?" | Asks "how has this worked for others like us?" |
| Pushes back on logic | Pushes back on fit or trust |
| Minimal relationship-building before getting to business | Opens with personal context before business |
| "What does this get me?" | "What's it like to work with you?" |

### J vs. P (Judging vs. Perceiving — how structured their process is)
| J signals | P signals |
|---|---|
| Has an internal timeline and process already | "We're not in a rush, still figuring it out" |
| Asks procedural questions early | Flexible, can adapt timeline easily |
| Wants the plan before committing | Open to figuring it out together |
| "Walk me through your onboarding" | "What do most clients start with?" |

### E vs. I (Extrovert vs. Introvert — energy and information processing)
| E signals | I signals |
|---|---|
| Talks through ideas out loud | Quiet, processes before responding |
| Immediate reactions and follow-up questions | Pauses before answering |
| Long answers to open-ended questions | Short, precise answers |
| Brings in other people, references conversations | "I've been thinking about this" (solo) |

### S vs. N (Sensing vs. Intuition — concrete vs. abstract)
| S signals | N signals |
|---|---|
| Asks about specific deliverables and timelines | Asks about strategy and possibilities |
| References past experience ("we tried X") | References future vision ("what if we could...") |
| Needs proof of concept and case studies | Excited by the idea before the evidence |
| Detail-oriented, sequential | Big picture first, details as needed |

## Behavioral Driver Reads (Terry Bean Framework)

### Fire (drive to acquire)
- Win-language: "get," "close," "grow," "beat"
- Short sentences. Interrupts. Drives to decision.
- Low tolerance for process talk before outcome talk.
- Types that lead here: Commander, Executive, Entrepreneur

### Earth (drive to defend)
- Stability-language: "proven," "reliable," "track record," "exactly what we'd get"
- Process-oriented. Reads contracts. Needs to understand every step.
- Long evaluation periods are normal — not disinterest.
- Types that lead here: Logistician, Defender, Executive

### Water (drive to bond)
- Relationship-language: "team," "partner," "we," "trust"
- Opens with personal context. References relationships.
- ~50% of buyers have Water as primary driver.
- Types that lead here: Protagonist, Consul, Defender, Advocate

### Air (drive to learn)
- Curiosity-language: "interesting," "I've been thinking," "what if," "why"
- Idea-oriented, can lose track of decision process.
- Needs intellectual engagement before emotional buy-in.
- Types that lead here: Architect, Logician, Debater, Campaigner

## Red Flags — Signals That Should Pause the Profile

**Conflicting signals:** T-dominant language with strong Water driver showing up. Hold — get more signal before committing to a type.

**Performed language:** Some prospects perform the type they think they should be in a meeting. A Defender might open like a Commander to appear decisive. Check consistency across the call — type shows up in unguarded moments, not polished opens.

**Third-party input:** "My partner thinks..." or "Our CEO said..." = hidden stakeholders. The person you're profiling may not be the decision-maker. Profile the room before the call, not just the person on the screen.

**Thin transcripts:** Under five minutes of substantive content, or a mostly-logistics call, is not enough signal. File a Low-confidence working hypothesis and build a probe question set instead.
